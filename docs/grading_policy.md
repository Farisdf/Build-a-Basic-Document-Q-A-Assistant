# CS 201 — Grading and Assessment Policy

## Grade breakdown

The final course grade is made up of four components:

- Four programming assignments, worth 40% of the final grade in total (10% each).
- Weekly lab exercises, worth 10% of the final grade in total. Each of the 12 labs is
  marked as complete or incomplete, and the lab mark is the percentage of labs completed.
- One midterm exam, worth 20% of the final grade. The midterm is held during the
  Wednesday lecture slot in Week 7 (22 October 2025) and lasts 75 minutes.
- One final exam, worth 30% of the final grade. The final exam is 2 hours long and is
  scheduled by the registrar during the December examination period.

## Passing requirements

To pass CS 201 a student must achieve an overall grade of at least 50% AND score at least
40% on the final exam. A student who scores below 40% on the final exam will receive a
maximum course grade of 45%, regardless of their assignment marks.

## Letter grade scale

Overall percentages are converted to letter grades as follows: A is 85% and above,
B is 70% to 84%, C is 55% to 69%, D is 50% to 54%, and F is below 50%.

## Late submission policy

Programming assignments are due at 11:59 pm on the stated due date. Late assignments lose
5% of the assignment's maximum mark for every 24 hours (or part thereof) that they are late.
Assignments submitted more than 5 days late receive a mark of zero. Lab exercises cannot be
submitted late because they are marked during the lab session itself.

## Extensions and special consideration

Students may request an extension of up to 7 days for one programming assignment per
semester without providing documentation; this must be requested through the course
website at least 48 hours before the deadline. Longer extensions, or extensions on more than
one assignment, require a medical certificate or a letter from the Student Wellbeing Office.
Missed exams are handled through the Faculty's special consideration process, and a
missed midterm with an approved reason has its weight transferred to the final exam.

## Academic integrity

All submitted code must be the student's own work. Students may discuss approaches with
classmates but must not share code. Use of AI code generators must be declared in a comment
at the top of each submitted file. Suspected plagiarism is referred to the Faculty
Academic Integrity Committee, and a first confirmed offence results in a mark of zero for
the assessment concerned.
